# Findings

## Plan
1. Compute overall churn and churn by every column (not only onboarding).
2. Compare effect sizes; check for confounding by splitting onboarding within price-notice groups.
3. Note limits of the exit survey (it only reached churned customers).

## Results
Overall churn: 487 of 3000 (16.2%).

| Column | Churn rate by value |
|---|---|
| price_increase_notice | no 5.2% (n=1860), yes 34.2% (n=1140): gap 29 points |
| onboarding_completed | no 17.6% (n=1339), yes 15.2% (n=1661): gap 2.4 points |
| plan | basic 16.7%, pro 15.3%, team 16.4% |
| signup_month | 14.5% to 19.3%, no pattern |

Within each price-notice group the onboarding gap stays small: 6.5% vs 4.2% without the notice, 35.2% vs 33.4% with it.

The exit survey (68% of churned customers called onboarding confusing) asked only people who had already left. Without a survey of retained customers, we cannot tell whether they find onboarding just as confusing; it does not show that onboarding causes churn.

## Conclusion
Churn is driven by the July 2025 price-increase notice. Completing onboarding is associated with only about 2 points less churn. Rebuilding onboarding would not address most churn; a response to the price notice (messaging, grandfathering, retention offers) should be examined first.

Main driver: price_increase_notice
Onboarding effect: small
